# Forms, input and actions

Read when you build fields, validation, error messages, buttons, or a flow with several steps.

## Fewer, easier fields

- Ask only for what the task needs; every extra field adds load. `[Cognitive load]`
- Prefill and autofill what the system knows or can work out, so tasks take no longer than people expect. `[Tesler]` `[Parkinson]`
- Don't make people remember things between steps or screens; carry the information along. `[Working memory]`
- Mark optional fields as optional. In one test, a visible "optional" on a phone field doubled sign-ups (40% to 80%). `[Myth: small details don't matter]`
- Let people do the main task before any setup or sign-up they don't need yet (letting shoppers skip sign-up added about $300 million a year). `[Myth: small details don't matter]`

## Input, errors and help

- Accept input in the forms people naturally type, convert it to what the system needs, set clear limits, and show what was understood. `[Postel]`
- Make error messages specific and helpful; better error messages raised completed purchases and saved over £250,000 a year in one shop. `[Myth: small details don't matter]`
- Put help where it's needed (hints, examples, tooltips), not in a manual. People start using a product without reading instructions. `[Paradox of the active user]` `[Tesler]`

## Buttons and targets

- Make targets big enough to hit accurately, with space between them, near where eyes and hands already are. `[Fitts]`
- Keep destructive actions away from frequent ones; fast moves to small targets cause errors. `[Fitts]`
- Say plainly what a destructive action will lose; losses weigh about twice as much as gains. `[Loss aversion]`

## Longer flows

- Split complex tasks into smaller steps. `[Hick]` `[Chunking]`
- Show progress and the progress already made. People speed up as the end comes into view, and unfinished tasks stay on their minds. `[Goal-gradient]` `[Zeigarnik]`
- Give clear step-by-step guidance and keep effort reasonable. People value what they helped make, but only when they manage to finish it. `[IKEA effect]`
- Match the difficulty to people's skill and remove friction: too hard frustrates, too easy bores. `[Flow]`
