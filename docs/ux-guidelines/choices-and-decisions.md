# Choices, defaults and numbers

Read when people pick between options, accept a default or recommendation, compare things, or read numbers to decide.

## Options

- Fewer options mean faster decisions. When time matters, limit them and mark the recommended one, but don't simplify until the screen is vague. `[Hick]`
- Many options overwhelm: about 30% used a coupon to buy after seeing 6 jams vs 3% after 24. Show what matters now first, and offer filters and search to narrow down. `[Choice overload]`
- When people must compare, show the options side by side. `[Choice overload]`
- More features don't make people happier. Satisfaction after use comes from usability, and features are hard to take away, so add them carefully. `[Myth: more choices and features]`
- Don't cut a list to 7 by rule; options on screen don't need memorising. Keep each one only if it's needed. `[Myth: 7±2]` `[Miller]`
- Decisions get worse after many in a row. Cut the number of decisions with good defaults and automate routine ones. `[Decision fatigue]`

## Defaults and recommendations

- People mostly keep the default, so make it the option that serves them and is safe. `[Status quo]`
- The first number or option people see anchors what follows. Give more than one reference point, and never an arbitrary one. `[Anchoring]`
- People accept what looks authoritative without checking it. Show the reason for a recommendation, not just a badge. `[Authority bias]`

## Wording a choice

- Describe options evenly: one treatment got 72% support when framed as lives saved and 22% as lives lost. Never steer with guilt or fear. `[Framing]`
- Show what each option involves (cost, time, what happens next); people avoid choices whose outcome is unclear. `[Ambiguity effect]`
- No false urgency ("last chance") to play on fear of regret; show realistic outcomes instead. `[Regret aversion]`
- Use a third, weaker option only to help people find what fits them, never to push the pricier one. `[Decoy]`
- Showing what others do is persuasive (6% followed a sign alone, 67% after seeing two people do it). Use it honestly and leave people free to choose. `[Social norms]`
- People favour now over later. When asking for effort today, make the later benefit concrete. `[Hyperbolic discounting]`
- People don't weigh options rationally; decoys, anchors and "free" shift choices predictably. Test choice screens with real people. `[Myth: people are rational]`

## Numbers and data

- Show the total or the usual rate next to a single case, so one vivid example doesn't dominate. `[Base rate]`
- Count what dropped out: include failed, stopped or removed items and show the denominator. `[Survivorship]`
- Show gains and losses in the same frame, and show a cost or consequence at the moment people decide. `[Framing]` `[Salience]`
- Bad news outweighs good news of the same size, so keep warning colours for real problems. `[Negativity bias]`
