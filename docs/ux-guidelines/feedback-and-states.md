# Feedback, waits and states

Read when you design loading, waiting, progress, empty, error or success states, or the end of a flow.

## Speed

- Respond to every action within 400 ms, even if the work takes longer. `[Doherty]`
- Make waits feel shorter: show useful content or animation while loading, and a progress indicator for long waits, even a rough one. `[Doherty]`
- A visible pause can make a complex result feel more trustworthy. Use it only when it helps people trust the result, never to look busy. `[Doherty]`
- Finish sooner than people expect where you can; work stretches to fill the time it's given. `[Parkinson]`

## States

- **Empty:** say what will appear and offer the next step. New users act before they read help, so guide them in place. `[Paradox of the active user]`
- **More to see:** make further content easy to spot, with clear signals that invite people to explore it. `[Zeigarnik]`
- **Error:** say what happened and how to fix it, where it happened. Bad moments are remembered most vividly. `[Peak-end]` `[Negativity bias]`
- **Success:** confirm what happened and what comes next. `[Peak-end]`
- **Feedback:** show people what they've done and achieved, so they keep a sense of control. `[Flow]`

## Progress and endings

- Show progress toward a goal. People speed up near the end and remember what's unfinished. `[Goal-gradient]` `[Zeigarnik]`
- People judge an experience by its peak and its end. Find the most valuable moment and make it great, and end every flow clearly. `[Peak-end]`
- Make costs and consequences visible when they happen, not hidden. `[Salience]`
- Aim past "usable": good experiences also feel good, so add personality where it fits. `[Myth: UX is about usability]`
