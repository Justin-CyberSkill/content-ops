# Research, testing and metrics

Read when you check a design, ask users for input, read feedback or metrics, or decide whether to redesign.

## You are not the user

- Builders know too much to judge what's clear, and assume others think as they do. Design for outsiders and check with real target users. `[Myth: you are like your users]` `[False consensus]`
- An expert review is quick and catches obvious problems, but experts predict users poorly; follow it with a test. `[Myth: experts don't need to test]`
- Don't copy a pattern because a big product uses it. Ask why it works there, and test it here. `[Myth: if it works for Amazon]`

## Watch, don't ask

- Watch what people do; it often differs from what they say (only 25–27% like the dark roast they say they want). Don't ask people to predict their future use. `[Myth: people can tell you what they want]`
- People use products in ways nobody planned. Collect evidence of real use. `[Myth: people use it as you imagined]`
- A usability test (one person doing tasks) and a focus group (people talking) answer different questions. `[Myth: testing = focus groups]`
- Testing is cheap: 3 to 5 people find most of the big problems, and paper prototypes are enough. `[Myth: testing is expensive]`
- Ask neutral questions, keep surveys short, offer a middle option, and don't reveal the answer you hope for. `[Response bias]`
- Don't cue participants; your expectations change how they behave. `[Observer expectancy]`
- A polished prototype hides usability problems in a test. `[Aesthetic-usability]`

## Reading results

- Look for evidence that you're wrong, not only for what confirms your idea. `[Confirmation bias]`
- Count what dropped out, failed or stopped, not just what survived. `[Survivorship]`
- Compare with the usual rate before reacting to one vivid case. `[Base rate]`
- You value what you built; judge your design by the same criteria as anyone else's. `[IKEA effect]`
- Knowing a bias doesn't remove it, so build checks into the process. `[Cognitive bias]`
- Estimates run late. Base them on how long similar work really took, and split work into small parts. `[Planning fallacy]`

## Pace

- Success takes iteration; don't judge a change by its first results. `[Myth: success happens overnight]`
- UX isn't one step in a project. It runs from strategy through launch and after. `[Myth: UX is a step]`
- Design is how it works, not just how it looks. `[Myth: design is about looks]`
