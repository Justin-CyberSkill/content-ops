# AI and agents

Read when an agent or AI makes, suggests, changes or does anything people see, and when you design how people review it. Agents propose; people decide. The evidence below is why review has to be designed, not assumed.

## Showing AI work

- People accept automated suggestions even against the evidence in front of them (59% accuracy with an automated aid vs 97% without, in one study). Show the reasoning and sources behind each suggestion so people can check it. `[Automation bias]`
- Show uncertainty instead of hiding it: confidence, alternatives, or what the AI didn't know. `[AI literacy gap]`
- Put a prompt to check next to high-stakes AI content. `[AI literacy gap]` `[Automation bias]`
- Label AI work as AI work. Don't use human-like cues that overstate its understanding or care. `[Parasocial trust in AI]`
- Don't give AI output authority cues (expert badges, confident styling) it hasn't earned; people should judge the content, not the messenger. `[Authority bias]`
- One good result doesn't make the next one good; let people judge each output on its own. `[Halo effect]`

## Reviewing

- Make overriding cheap and normal: rejecting or editing is as easy as accepting, and corrections count as good use, not failure. `[Automation bias]` `[AI literacy gap]`
- Where it matters, let people see the source or form their own view before the AI's answer, so it doesn't anchor them. `[Anchoring]` `[Automation bias]`
- Give each AI-shaped decision a named human owner, shown with the decision. `[Accountability diffusion]`
- Add friction and human review to high-stakes actions. `[Parasocial trust in AI]` `[Delegation creep]`
- Long review queues wear decisions down; keep them short and let people stop and come back. `[Decision fatigue]`

## How much agents may do

- Sort tasks by stakes: what an agent may do alone, what needs review, and red lines that stay human. Widen an agent's scope only by an explicit decision. `[Delegation creep]`
- Audit what agents actually do: sample outputs and track error and override rates. `[Delegation creep]` `[Automation bias]`
